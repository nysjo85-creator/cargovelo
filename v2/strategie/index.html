1. Positioning: ontzorgen, not bezorgen

Cargo Velo does not sell a delivery. It sells the end of a worry: the sample arrives cold and on time, the webshop customer is happy, the urgent part is on site, the CO2 report is done. "Ontzorgen" is the word customers already use: a Google review from Au Pain Marie says "Zodat wij ontzorgd zijn en de klant toch alles goed krijgt." Mokabon calls Cargo Velo "het verlengstuk van ons merk".

Positioning statement (draft). Cargo Velo is the emission-free logistics partner that takes the worry out of moving what matters in Gent, Antwerpen and Brussel: from webshop parcels and urgent parts to medical samples and DNA. One contact, proof of every delivery, zero emissions.

Message pillars

  • Eén aanspreekpunt. A fixed contact who starts up and follows every shipment (already promised on the Care page).
  • Op tijd, en bewezen. 99% first attempt, NPS 94, track and trace, proof of delivery.
  • Zonder uitstoot. 100% emission-free, with CO2 data for the customer's reporting.

Wording shifts

Today"Onze fietskoeriers bezorgen al jouw pakketten."
Proposed"Wij ontzorgen, jij groeit."
Today"Spoedkoerier: 30 minuten na pick-up."
Proposed"Spoed? Wij ontzorgen binnen 30 minuten."

Position the company as an enabler of amazing organisations (hospitals, labs, retailers, caterers, logistics partners), and tell stories through them. The cinematic hero video (couriers through Brussels at golden hour) supports this: human, calm, craft, city.

Beyond the homepage

  • Give express, medical samples and DNA/biotech their own pages, as Jo asked. They are today's most defensible and highest-value work and are buried on the Care page.
  • Keep documents and internal mail as one small tile. It is a real use case (COCOF), but it should not define the company.

2. Three cities, without feeling limiting

The site mentions the cities but never explains the model. The model is a strength: three dense city networks, connected by a shuttle, delivering in the cities and the surroundings. v2 shows this as "Drie steden. Eén netwerk."

Sequence

  • Months 0 to 6, package and prove. Express, medical and biotech pages; city pages and Google Business Profiles; compliance pack; price tiers; outreach to hospitals, labs and carriers; confirm the revenue baseline by line.
  • Months 6 to 12, scale. Convert first wins to recurring contracts; English pages; publish case studies; add inter-city shuttle rates; train couriers for medical rounds.
  • Months 12 to 18, extend. Add routes and couriers where contracts demand them; consider Flemish city pilots through zero-emission-zone programmes, last because it needs the most capital.

Tailwinds

  • Ghent's low-emission zone regulation was updated in December 2025 (fine 185 euro). Brussels banned Euro 5 diesel in January 2026. Flanders is working on zero-emission zones for city logistics.
  • CSRD scope was narrowed by the Omnibus, so fewer shippers are legally forced to request CO2 data. It is still a sales tool for large customers' Scope 3 reporting, but not a growth engine on its own.

Track

  • Revenue per courier-day, share of recurring revenue, revenue by line, first-attempt rate, NPS, number of active hospital, lab and carrier contracts, qualified inbound requests by page.

4. Which queries to cover

You asked ChatGPT for a delivery in Ghent and Cargo Velo came up. That fits what the desk research found: Cargo Velo shows up through the city of Ghent's last-mile provider list and press coverage, rather than through cargovelo.be itself. To be recommended for the rest of the business, each business line needs its own page and its own proof.

What the search check showed

  • cargovelo.be itself did not surface for the tested queries. Cargo Velo appeared through the Ghent last-mile provider list and press about the merger.
  • No dedicated medical bike courier appeared for Ghent or Brussels, and nothing Belgian appeared for DNA sample courier, so those are open fields.
  • For urgent same-day queries, brokers such as Dagoexpress and Eurosender, and bike couriers in Brussels, dominated.

Gaps on the current site

  • Only Dutch and French. There is no English page, which matters for biotech and international clients.
  • No city pages, no standalone express page, and "health and food" bundles lab samples with flowers, catering and mail. The page never says "DNA".
  • No visible FAQ and no FAQ markup. The structured data lists only the Ghent address and has no areaServed, so Antwerp and Brussels are invisible to search engines and AI assistants.

Top ten, in order

  • 1. Express page. 2. Three city pages with hub, shuttle and FAQ. 3. areaServed plus a place entity per hub. 4. Medical page separate from food. 5. Biotech page in Dutch and English. 6. Webshop and returns page. 7. Visible FAQ and FAQ markup on every page. 8. Google Business Profiles for Antwerp and Brussels. 9. Partner page, plus listings with the Belgian Cycle Logistics Federation and Cairgobike. 10. A Wikidata entry and a press page linking the merger and the Revolve cover story.

v2 now follows this structure. It is 16 separate pages instead of one long scroll: a short home page, one page per service (Haast, medical samples, DNA and biotech, sensitive deliveries, vaste rondes, logistics partners, webshops and retail), a page for the three-city network plus one each for Gent, Antwerpen and Brussel, customers, sustainability, about and the request form. Each page has its own title, description, breadcrumb, an FAQ built from these queries and the matching Service, FAQPage and LocalBusiness markup with areaServed. The pages are set to noindex while this is a prototype. When it goes live, remove noindex and add canonical URLs, a sitemap and hreflang for the Dutch, French and English versions.

Knowing who clicks what

Separate pages also show the path to "Plan je levering". Every call-to-action carries its source, and the request email includes the page the visitor clicked from and the pages they viewed in that session (kept in the browser until they send a request, and mentioned in the form's fine print). Connect a privacy-friendly analytics tool later to read the same click events.

Off-site sources that AI assistants and Google tend to cite

The city of Ghent's last-mile provider list (already working), the Cycle Logistics Federation, Google Business Profiles, directories, Wikidata, and press such as the merger coverage and the Revolve cover story. Ask Antwerp and Brussels for equivalent provider listings.
